\label{sec:conclusion}

In this report, we failed to reproduce the results presented in \cite{sinha2023dasmcc}; however, we found evidence in their paper that led to their SoTA results.
Additionally, we identify critical errors throughout their paper, as raised in \textbf{Sec. \ref{sec:part_1_discrepancy}}. Therefore, though beyond the purpose of study, we call for an in–depth revision of this paper \cite{sinha2023dasmcc}.
From the evidence above, e.g. a balanced test set of $9{,}083$ records, we believe they applied \texttt{SMOTE} before the train/test split, introducing data leakage to their test set, and thus, ideal results were obtained.
To verify this conjecture, we also conduct an ablation study by leaking data in the same way, and obtain close, though not identical, results, i.e. $80.5$–$88.4\%$ accuracy for the ensembles against their $88.0$–$93.0\%$.

Having criticised their paper, we shall propose our Machine Learning solution to the same task, though we compare it to the reproduced results, not their presented results.
In our solution, we leverage a 1D CNN to extract hidden features that may not be captured by the hand–crafted features extracted by \texttt{neurokit2}.
We then train an Autoencoder, and utilise its Encoder to compress the $2{,}225$ concatenated features into a $16$–dimensional latent vector, which is fed to the same set of classifiers used in \cite{sinha2023dasmcc}.
Empirically, the results are only above–average, the ensembles reaching $56.8$–$58.4$ F1, up from $39.2$–$40.5$ in the reproduction, and thus, require more enhancements to reach product quality.
Remarkably, we also conduct an ablation study to compare whether compressed or uncompressed features benefit the selected classifiers the most.
Interestingly, the tree–based models gain $8.7$–$18.7$ F1 points by directly training and testing on uncompressed data, at an expense of longer training and inference time. Meanwhile, due to the Curse of Dimensionality, \texttt{KNN} (a distance–based classifier) performs poorly on uncompressed data, dropping from $47.9$ to $38.9$ F1.
